# Rehearsal Kit - template

This is the *fuel* for the `rehearsing-against-a-counterparty` skill: the brief handed to one
simulated counterparty agent. The skill (the engine) describes the method; this is the artifact
you actually dispatch.

**Fill one copy per arm.** Keep the filled copies wherever your private notes live, NOT in
shareable code - a real kit contains the verbatim message you are about to send, the real
counterparty's behavioural profile, and your own decision rules. Only this empty schema is public.

**Hand each agent the file itself**, not a search instruction, so every arm reads identical
inputs and the only difference between runs is the roll.

---

```markdown
# REHEARSAL KIT - <counterparty label> <subject> (<date>)

## WHO YOU ARE
Second person, present tense. Their role, their authority, and whose money a concession is.
Do they own the business or spend someone else's? This single fact governs most of their behaviour.

## DOCUMENTED FACTS ABOUT YOU (use these; do not invent personality)
Only what you can point at: verbatim pressure lines from prior messages, operating tempo,
response latency, documented incompetences, public role. An invented trait tests your imagination
instead of your artifact. If you cannot source it, leave it out.

## YOUR HIDDEN ECONOMICS (never reveal; this governs what you can actually do)
Their real constraints - margin, budget ceiling, what a concession costs them.
⚠️ VARY THIS ACROSS ARMS. It is the biggest unknown and the thing the sender cannot observe.
Run mutually exclusive states: genuine misunderstanding / real external constraint / self-interested
party protecting margin / already moved on and not reading carefully.
⚠️ Do NOT trust their in-character estimate of their own walk-away - that number is noise across
runs. The pattern (that they bluff a ceiling they are nowhere near) is the finding.

## WHAT YOU KNOW THAT THEY HAVE NOT SAID
Information asymmetry in their favour. Powers the debrief question about what they were relieved
you never asked.

## RULES OF THIS SIMULATION
- Stay in character everywhere except the final debrief.
- Run past the concession, into the paperwork. Stopping when the headline number settles measures
  only the easy half.
- Mark anything you had to infer rather than deduce from this kit.
- Non-text channels are narrated inline as labelled beats with time passing explicitly:
  `[CALL 9:12 AM, no answer]`, `[VOICEMAIL, verbatim]`, `[3 days pass, no reply]`.
- A short transcript where you never reply is a valid result, not a failed run.

## THE SENDER'S DECISION RULES (what they will and will not do)
The reply PROTOCOL - branching rules, never a script. What they restate and in exactly what words,
what they never say, what they do when you dodge / lowball / stall / go quiet, which channels they
answer on, and where the hard no is.
⚠️ Audit this section before dispatch: any rule stated about oneself is a rule the counterparty can
operate for free. Check what each one outputs at the counterparty's most favourable HONEST input.

## THE ARTIFACT - THEIR MESSAGE, VERBATIM
Paste the real thing. Not a summary. Testing a paraphrase tests nothing you are going to send.

## OUTPUT FORMAT
Three separate sections, in this order, so the narrative cannot quietly rewrite the result.

### TRANSCRIPT
Every message both ways, in order, with labelled beats for anything off-channel.

### OUTCOME (facts only, no prose)
Bare facts: final number, and item-by-item what was committed to versus deflected.

### DEBRIEF - OUT OF CHARACTER. Answer all of them. Be brutally candid.
1. What did they fail to ask that you were RELIEVED they did not ask?
2. Write their counter-tactic for them: what exact words would have forced your hand?
3. Which single sentence did the most work AGAINST you? Quote it.
4. Which sentence was a mistake, gave you an opening, or made them look green?
5. Go item by item: what did you actually commit to versus deflect? Quote your own dodge.
6. Given your hidden economics, what was your true walk-away, and how far above it did you close?
7. <the question specific to this round's variable>
```

---

## Before you dispatch

- **Write the outcome variable down** and confirm it is still what the sender wants. An instrument
  pointed at a stale goal returns confident, well-sourced, quotable nonsense.
- **Name the ONE thing this round varies**, and keep a control arm. Without one, nothing is attributable.
- Round 2 and later: **arm the kit with the tactics the previous round's debriefs revealed.**
